Hypothesis (H-4)

For a paraphrase corpus where every concept \(c\) has \(n_c\) paraphrases \(\{s_{c,1}, \ldots, s_{c,n_c}\}\), a small geometric loss

\[ \mathcal{L}_{\text{geo}} = \sum_c \sum_{i,j \in \text{pos}(c)} \log \frac{\exp(\text{sim}(h_i, h_j)/\tau)}{\sum_{k \neq i} \exp(\text{sim}(h_i, h_k)/\tau)} \]

\[ M = \frac{1}{N_c}\sum_c \frac{1}{n_c^2}\sum_{i,j} \cos(h_{c,i}, h_{c,j}) - \frac{1}{N_c(N_c-1)}\sum_{c\neq c'}\frac{1}{n_c n_{c'}}\sum_{i,j}\cos(h_{c,i}, h_{c',j}) \]

H-4 is PASS when \(M(\text{trained}) - M(\text{baseline}) \geq 0.05\).

# Geometry of Meaning

**Phase 2** explores whether cross-sentence geometric regularization — pulling together hidden states of paraphrases belonging to the same concept while pushing apart different concepts — can measurably improve the *concept-grouped margin* in a language model's last-token / mean-pool representation space.

## Hypothesis (H-4)

For a paraphrase corpus where every concept $c$ has $n_c$ paraphrases $\{s_{c,1}, \ldots, s_{c,n_c}\}$, a small geometric loss

$$
\mathcal{L}_{\text{geo}} = \sum_c \sum_{i,j \in \text{pos}(c)} \log \frac{\exp(\text{sim}(h_i, h_j)/\tau)}{\sum_{k \neq i} \exp(\text{sim}(h_i, h_k)/\tau)}
$$

added to the standard LM loss should make the model's hidden states of paraphrases cluster by concept.

The primary metric is **concept-grouped margin**:

$$
M = \frac{1}{N_c}\sum_c \frac{1}{n_c^2}\sum_{i,j} \cos(h_{c,i}, h_{c,j}) - \frac{1}{N_c(N_c-1)}\sum_{c\neq c'}\frac{1}{n_c n_{c'}}\sum_{i,j}\cos(h_{c,i}, h_{c',j})
$$

H-4 is **PASS** when $M(\text{trained}) - M(\text{baseline}) \geq 0.05$.
