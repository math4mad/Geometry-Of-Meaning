0.5B v1-v3 — Cross-Sentence InfoNCE

0.5B Stage: From FAIL to PASS

All 0.5B experiments use Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct + LoRA (r=16, α=32), MPS (Apple Silicon), 2 epochs, 50 paraphrase concepts × 5 paraphrases.

v1: Block-Internal Geo Loss — FAIL

Loss design: for each sentence, take the last-layer hidden state, divide into two halves along the sequence axis, and apply InfoNCE between the two halves.

Why it failed: the proxy optimises token-level smoothness within one sentence, not cross-sentence concept alignment. The geometric loss did decrease (0.55 → 0.49), but the concept margin collapsed:

Pool baseline λ=0 λ=0.05 λ=0.2 λ=1.0
last 0.030 0.019 0.019 0.019 0.016
mean 0.043 0.037 0.036 0.035 0.038

H-4 FAIL on both pools.

v2: Micro-Batch Only — FAIL (NaN)

Loss design: replace the LM stream entirely with paraphrase micro-batches.

Why it failed: the LM loss exploded to 6.85 (v1 was 2.5), and InfoNCE returned NaN. The training distribution was entirely off the LM manifold.

v3: LM Stream + Periodic Cross-Sentence InfoNCE — PASS

Loss design: keep the LM stream block (128 tokens, standard causal LM), and every \(G=4\) LM steps pause to do one cross-sentence micro-batch:

  • \(K=4\) concepts drawn from the 50
  • 2 concepts have 2 paraphrases each (provides 1 positive pair per concept)
  • 2 concepts have 1 paraphrase each (negative-only contribution)
  • Total: 6 sentences, 2 positive pairs, 12 inter-concept negative pairs
H-4 result:
                last_token   mean_pool
baseline          0.0295       0.0426
λ=0 (LoRA only)   0.0194       0.0371
λ=0.05            0.0249       0.0390
λ=0.2             0.0431       0.0453
λ=1.0             0.1601       0.1035
best Δ           +0.131 PASS  +0.061 PASS

InfoNCE dropped from 1.26 → 0.70 across 2 epochs (λ=1, last_token), confirming the proxy loss is genuinely training the alignment.

Diagnostic

Why v3 worked where v1 didn’t: the cross-sentence positive pairs are the same concept in different surface forms — exactly the alignment we want. v1’s “positive pairs” were two halves of the same sentence — a totally different signal.

Why v3 worked where v2 didn’t: keeping the LM stream maintains the language modelling gradient; v2’s pure-paraphrase batches stranded the model off-distribution.

# 0.5B Stage: From FAIL to PASS

All 0.5B experiments use Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct + LoRA (r=16, α=32), MPS (Apple Silicon), 2 epochs, 50 paraphrase concepts × 5 paraphrases.

## v1: Block-Internal Geo Loss — FAIL

Loss design: for each sentence, take the last-layer hidden state, divide into two halves along the sequence axis, and apply InfoNCE *between the two halves*.

**Why it failed**: the proxy optimises token-level smoothness *within* one sentence, not cross-sentence concept alignment. The geometric loss did decrease (0.55 → 0.49), but the concept margin collapsed:

| Pool | baseline | λ=0 | λ=0.05 | λ=0.2 | λ=1.0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| last | 0.030 | 0.019 | 0.019 | 0.019 | 0.016 |
| mean | 0.043 | 0.037 | 0.036 | 0.035 | 0.038 |

**H-4 FAIL** on both pools.

## v2: Micro-Batch Only — FAIL (NaN)

Loss design: replace the LM stream entirely with paraphrase micro-batches.

**Why it failed**: the LM loss exploded to 6.85 (v1 was 2.5), and InfoNCE returned NaN. The training distribution was entirely off the LM manifold.

## v3: LM Stream + Periodic Cross-Sentence InfoNCE — **PASS**

Loss design: keep the LM stream block (128 tokens, standard causal LM), and every $G=4$ LM steps pause to do one cross-sentence micro-batch:

- **$K=4$ concepts** drawn from the 50
- **2 concepts** have 2 paraphrases each (provides 1 positive pair per concept)
- **2 concepts** have 1 paraphrase each (negative-only contribution)
- Total: **6 sentences**, **2 positive pairs**, 12 inter-concept negative pairs

```
H-4 result:
                last_token   mean_pool
baseline          0.0295       0.0426
λ=0 (LoRA only)   0.0194       0.0371
λ=0.05            0.0249       0.0390
λ=0.2             0.0431       0.0453
λ=1.0             0.1601       0.1035
best Δ           +0.131 PASS  +0.061 PASS
```

InfoNCE dropped from 1.26 → 0.70 across 2 epochs (λ=1, last_token), confirming the proxy loss is genuinely training the alignment.

## Diagnostic

**Why v3 worked where v1 didn't**: the cross-sentence positive pairs are *the same concept in different surface forms* — exactly the alignment we want. v1's "positive pairs" were two halves of *the same sentence* — a totally different signal.

**Why v3 worked where v2 didn't**: keeping the LM stream maintains the language modelling gradient; v2's pure-paraphrase batches stranded the model off-distribution.
